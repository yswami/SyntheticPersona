# Guardrails

## Appropriate uses

- Rehearsing an evaluation before customer sessions
- Stress-testing workflow assumptions
- Generating candidate failure modes and edge cases
- Improving discussion guides and research questions
- Comparing how explicitly defined behavioral lenses interpret the same workflow
- Testing whether an optional OCEAN variation changes style without inventing facts or authority

## Inappropriate uses

- Declaring customer validation or product-market fit
- Replacing usability testing or observation of real behavior
- Estimating willingness to pay without market evidence
- Making high-stakes employment, financial, legal, medical, safety, or access decisions
- Fabricating participant quotations, prevalence, or statistical confidence
- Simulating a real identifiable individual without authorization
- Treating an assumed personality profile as psychological assessment or customer evidence

## Data handling

- Do not enter confidential, customer-identifying, personal, regulated, or security-sensitive data into an unapproved service.
- Prefer fictional or sanitized examples for public demonstrations.
- Follow applicable organizational policy, consent, retention, and data-processing requirements.
- Do not publish internal research artifacts, screenshots, participant details, or proprietary workflow information without authorization.

## Reporting language

Use:

- “The simulated reviewer raised…”
- “This is a candidate risk…”
- “This produces a question for human research…”
- “The persona is based on assumed constraints…”

Do not use:

- “Customers said…”
- “Research proves…”
- “Users will…”
- “The persona validates…”

unless the statement is supported by appropriate real-world evidence.

## Evidence rules

- Every claimed research-grounded behavior needs an evidence reference.
- Generated text is never participant evidence.
- Analyst inference must be distinguishable from direct observation.
- Contradictory evidence and unknowns must remain visible.
- Archetypes must be revised when new evidence changes the pattern.

## Simulation rules

- Reveal only the information a real participant would have at that point.
- Permit reasonable, evidence-consistent inference but require assumptions to be labeled.
- Label OCEAN values as illustrative unless supported by an appropriate measurement process.
- Use personality only as a broad tendency layer; never infer domain knowledge, goals, decision rights, or product needs from it.
- Prevent the model from inventing product behavior, policy, organizational history, or prior experience.
- Keep the simulation in the participant lens rather than allowing it to become a generic design expert.
- Run material decisions through the three-question trust test: traceability, downstream impact, and decision authority.

## Publication checklist

- [ ] The example is fictional, sanitized, or explicitly authorized.
- [ ] Generated reactions are labeled as simulated.
- [ ] No employer, product, customer, participant, or roadmap information is implied.
- [ ] No output is presented as customer validation.
- [ ] Limitations and next human-validation steps are stated.
- [ ] Links point to content that is ready and useful to the public.
